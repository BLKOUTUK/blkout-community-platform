Slide 1 · Opening Users, not orgs
Beat 01 · Open slowly

Thank you. It's good to be in a room where nobody needs me to explain who I'm talking about.

I want to start with a quiet problem. Most of our organisations — yours, mine — can tell you who we reach. We know our members. We know our subscribers. We know who shows up.

What's harder is the other question. Who are we not reaching.

Beat 02 · Name the politics

At BLKOUT we noticed this properly last year. We were writing about Black queer mental health and we went looking for the numbers. There aren't any. Not really.

The census didn't publish the cross-tab. The NHS data doesn't hold the three things together. We're not under-counted — we're un-counted.

That's not a communications problem. It's a political one. If we're invisible in the data, we're invisible in the commissioning. We get designed out of the systems that are meant to serve us.

Beat 03 · Frame the talk

So this talk is about what we've been building with AI inside that problem. Not AI as a productivity story. AI as a way of reaching the people our own organisations keep missing.

Three examples. About seven minutes. And then an invitation.

BLKOUT UK is what Black queer men build when we stop waiting and start resisting.

We stopped waiting for the sector to catch up a long time ago. This is what we've been quietly making in the meantime.

Beat 04 · Ivor's Compass · ~2 min

Ivor Cummings was a civil servant at the Colonial Office in the 1940s and 50s. Gay, Black, the first person to meet the Empire Windrush when it docked. He's not in our movement's memory because he wasn't meant to be — the archives weren't built for him.

Last month we put 100 men through a one-day workshop based on his life. Men in their 40s, 50s, 60s, 70s. Men who don't come to our events. Men who are not on the scene.

Beat 05 · Compass · AI ≠ headline

The AI piece is not the headline. The headline is the seven directions of the compass — home, night, fire, threshold, shadow, silence, return — which came out of commissioned essays from four Black queer writers.

AI helped us turn those essays into a seven-table chatbot. Each table asks you a question about your own life. You answer. The responses get held in a journal you take home.

What this unlocked was heritage work for men who don't identify with the word "queer" and don't come to Pride. The AI didn't reach them. Ivor did. The AI just made a one-day workshop feel like six.

Beat 06 · Critical Frequency · ~90 sec

This one I'll say carefully, because it's the newest of the three and the least finished.

Every Pride season, our community gets a very particular kind of visibility. Feather boas. Glittery cowboy hats. A photo opportunity for the mayor. It's wonderful in its place — and it's not the visibility that ends up in a commissioner's spreadsheet.

What we're missing is the other kind. Decision-maker visibility. The cross-tab that makes us countable. The line item in the funding stream. The named intersection in the policy document.

Beat 07 · CF · Pride in London two-track

So Critical Frequency, at this stage, is not a tool. It's a frame. We're naming that gap publicly — it's where the last BMHWA webinar started — and we're sitting with the problem long enough to hypothesise some answers before we rush to build.

The next phase is a two-track campaign at Pride in London — feather boas at the front, numbers at the back. If we can get both showing up at the same time, that's how you move a commissioner.

Whatever AI ends up in the back end of that, it won't be the point. The point is the move from one visibility to the other.

Beat 08 · AIvor · ~90 sec

AIvor — with a capital A-I — is our platform assistant. Named after Ivor Cummings. He/him. 1940s voice.

This is where I'm most careful. AIvor is not a crisis service. He's not therapy. He's not a replacement for a person.

What he is, is a presence at 2am. When you've just had the thing happen that you can't phone anyone about. AIvor can listen. He can hand you to a human service when you're ready. And where it's safe to, he can carry a conversation across sessions, so you don't have to start again.

For us the AI question isn't can it do the work. It's can it hold the space until a human arrives. That turns out to be a much smaller, much more careful question than the sector usually asks.

Beat 09 · Bridge

Three things. Heritage. Policy. Platform. All three built around the same question — who are we not reaching — and all three costing less than one consultant's scoping document.

Here's what I'm here to say. We can't do this on our own. And nor should we.
Beat 10 · Name the co-op

Every organisation in this room is running into the same three problems. You want AI that's safe for your people. You can't afford to audit it properly. And you don't want to hand your community data to vendors who will sell it back to you next year.

So we're starting a Black Queer Liberation Tech Cooperative. Not a consortium. Not a think-tank. A co-op — member-owned, member-governed, with one goal: to put shared AI and data infrastructure under our collective control.

Beat 11 · Three first steps

One — an AI tool audit. We've built a tool called Dreamcatcher, with a judge inside it called Sylvia. Sylvia is our Reach Judge — she asks the questions about who a tool serves and who it misses. We'll run your stack through her, free, this summer. An honest answer, not a sales pitch.

Two — a tool-sharing protocol. A small, boring, important piece of governance. How do we share access to tools, data, and training across our organisations without one of us carrying the risk? We've got a v0 draft. We want you to break it.

Three — a hackathon for Black Pride. A working weekend before UK Black Pride in August. We ship one shared thing. Events registry. Safeguarding tool. Something we haven't thought of. You tell us.

Beat 12 · The URL · Sylvia

None of this requires you to sign anything today. The URL is up there. blkoutuk.com/commons. Five quick questions, a short registration of interest. If the answer is not yet, that's a full answer.

The take-home from this talk is that tool — Dreamcatcher, with Sylvia. Her questions are the political edge of what we do. She's there if you want her.

Beat 13 · Land it

BLKOUT is small. You are small. Our organisations will always be small, because we're built for specific people and we refuse to water that down.

Small isn't the problem to fix. The connections between us are the point.

That's the only reason I've kept going as long as I have. And it's the only reason I'm inviting you, today, to build this with us.

Thank you.
